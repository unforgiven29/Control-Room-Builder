How to use Control Board Layout

Getting started

  1. Each tab across the top is one board. Use + Board to add more, and click empty board space to set its size, paint and bench break.
  2. Pick a part on the left, then click the board to place it. Keep clicking to place more, and press Esc when you're done.
  3. Click a placed part to change its tag, nameplate and settings on the right. Drag it to move it.
  4. Switch to Room to arrange the boards in the control room, and Device list to check every tag at once.
  5. Use Export to save a layout file now and then, since your layout is only kept in this browser. Export also makes a PDF, a viewer page or a picture to send to friends, and Import opens a layout file someone sends you.

The board

  • The grid is ½ inch per square, so every part is drawn at its real size. Heavy lines are every inch unless you change it in Settings.
  • The silver rail splits the vertical section (meters, recorders, annunciators) from the bench board (switches). Drag it up or down.
  • A red dashed outline means a part overlaps another part, the rail or the edge. A red ! means its tag is used twice.

Working with many parts

  • Drag across empty board to select a group, or Shift-click parts. Drag any selected part to move them all.
  • With a group selected you can line them up, space them evenly, duplicate, copy, or save them as a section to reuse on other boards.
  • With one part selected, Make a row of copies fills a row of switches or lights in one step.

Measuring

  • Pick Measure at the top of the parts list, or press M, then drag between two points. The label shows the distance in feet and inches, plus the across and down distances.
  • Hold Shift to keep it straight, or Alt to measure to ⅛ in without snapping to the grid.

Mimics

  • Choose Mimic line, click to start, click each bend, then double-click or press Enter. Lines snap to the grid and run square.
  • Where lines cross, the drawing follows the ISO and NUREG convention: horizontal lines have priority and pass over, and the vertical line breaks with a small gap on each side. A line that only ends on another one (a tee or corner) stays joined.
  • Add mimic symbols (pumps, valves, tanks, breakers) along the lines. Filled symbols read as running or closed. Press R to turn a selected symbol, or Shift+R to turn it back.

Room

  • Drag boards to move them. [ and ] rotate them. Horseshoe wraps them around the operator.
  • Set each board's cabinet and bench depth so the floor plan is realistic. The room check warns about overlaps and tight aisles.

Real control room rules of thumb

  • Put controls between about 34 and 70 in above the floor, and keep the tops of meters at or below about 70 in so shorter operators can read them.
  • Annunciators go at the top of the vertical section, above the controls they relate to. Red trip and first-out windows usually go on the top row.
  • Group each system together: its title strip, its annunciator, its meters, then its switches below, with mimic lines tying them together.
  • Use pilot light colors consistently: red for running or closed, green for stopped or open.
  • Leave 36–48 in of aisle in front of boards, and either no gap or 24–36 in behind them for service.
  • Turn on height guides in Settings to see the reach and eye-level zones, and open Design check in a board's settings for a list of things to look at.

Heights come from US nuclear control room review guidance (NUREG-0700) and industry console recommendations; treat them as guides, not hard rules.
